What was a normal day like in the Aztec capital? In 1519, Spanish soldiers saw Tenochtitlan and wondered if they were dreaming. Let's follow one ordinary family from reed mats before sunrise to floating farms, the cleanest streets of the age, a giant market where cacao beans were money, school for every boy, strict rules about cloaks and alcohol, ball games, and the night the world could end.

Coatl and his family are fictional characters, but their day is built from historical sources.

FACTS FROM THIS VIDEO (with sources)
▸ Tenochtitlan was built on an island in Lake Texcoco (founded around 1325). Population estimates for 1519 vary a lot; 100,000–200,000 is a common range.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenochtitlan
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Texcoco
▸ Bernal Díaz del Castillo wrote that some soldiers asked whether what they saw was a dream.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernal_D%C3%ADaz_del_Castillo
▸ Most Aztecs ate two main meals a day. Grinding maize on a metate could take hours every day; corn was soaked in lime water (nixtamalization), and atole was drunk daily.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztec_cuisine
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metate
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nixtamalization
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atole
▸ Protein came from insects, water-fly eggs, axolotls, algae cakes (spirulina) and small dogs; turkeys were not a major part of the diet.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axolotl
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirulina_(dietary_supplement)
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xoloitzcuintle
▸ Chinampas were farm plots built in the shallow lake; some are still farmed in Xochimilco.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinampa
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xochimilco
▸ Many Aztecs bathed daily; Moctezuma II reportedly bathed twice a day. They used soap plants and steam baths (temazcal). Fresh water came by aqueduct from Chapultepec.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moctezuma_II
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temazcal
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapindus
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapultepec_aqueduct
▸ Cortés wrote that more than 60,000 people traded daily at the Tlatelolco market. Cacao beans were used as money, and some were counterfeited.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tlatelolco_(altepetl)
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hern%C3%A1n_Cort%C3%A9s
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocoa_bean
▸ Every boy went to school: the telpochcalli for commoners, the calmecac for nobles. The Codex Mendoza shows strict punishments, including chili smoke at age 11.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C4%93lpochcalli
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calmecac
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codex_Mendoza
▸ Commoners could not wear cotton or cloaks below the knee. Public drunkenness on pulque was punished; the elderly were allowed to drink.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztec_clothing
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulque
▸ Games: patolli (a board game played with beans) and the rubber ball game.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patolli
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerican_ballgame
▸ Two calendars (365 and 260 days) met every 52 years — the New Fire Ceremony. The last one was held in 1507.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztec_calendar
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Fire_ceremony
▸ Human sacrifice was part of Aztec religion; its scale is still debated.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_sacrifice_in_Aztec_culture
▸ Tenochtitlan fell in 1521. Over a million people still speak Nahuatl; chocolate, tomato, avocado, chili and coyote come from Nahuatl words.
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Tenochtitlan
  https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahuatl
